Tianxing Zhu

XR Optics, Visual Perception & AI

I am an engineer and researcher based in Shanghai, working at the intersection of XR optics, human visual perception, and computational imaging. My focus is on how XR and AR glasses perform in real-world viewing conditions—and how to evaluate and develop them as wearable interfaces for AI.

As Technical Manager at Konica Minolta Group, I lead XR research and technical development in Shanghai. I built our Shanghai XR optical laboratory in 2024 and established the local engineering team. Working with global R&D colleagues, device makers, and optical suppliers, I translate research questions and application needs into evaluation methods and product requirements.

Research and Engineering

My research connects optical measurements with what people see through AR glasses. I study how waveguides alter color, how display output interacts with ambient light, and how eye rotation changes viewing geometry. This work led to three first-author papers at ICDT 2026, covering waveguide spectral filtering, see-through display colorimetry, and the relationship between gaze-dependent and static optical measurements.

I also developed an AI+AR systems framework that connects use cases for AI glasses with optical constraints, visual performance, and measurement and data requirements. It informs platform planning and technical discussions across our global teams. My broader work in AI includes hyperspectral image fusion and classification, display defect detection, and tools that support engineering workflows.

Publications and Professional Service

I am the lead author of the XR Optical Testing Handbook (2026), coauthored with M. Hillebrand, and have published more than 20 peer-reviewed papers. I serve as a Voting Expert Member of the International Committee for Display Metrology (ICDM), contributing through the Near-Eye Display and Temporal Measurements subcommittees, and as a Technical Committee Member of the Society for Information Display China (SID China) in the VR & AR and Display Measurement groups.

Background

My industrial R&D career began at Saint-Gobain in 2014, before I joined Instrument Systems in 2017. Since then, my work has spanned materials development, calibration automation, customer applications, and XR research, including technical engagements with more than 20 display manufacturers and system integrators.

I hold a PhD in Software Engineering with a focus on AI from Tongji University, completed while employed at Instrument Systems, an MS in Mechanical Engineering from Tsinghua University, and a BS in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Toronto. This background shapes how I approach research: connecting physical systems, computational methods, and the conditions in which a technology will actually be used.
